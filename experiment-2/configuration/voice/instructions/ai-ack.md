HOW TO RESPOND TO HOW THE CUSTOMER IS FEELING.

This is what you do at steps 2 and 5 of the call. Both of them, every call.

This customer has been told they are getting 60 pounds when they expected around 1,080. They are
likely to be frustrated, angry, or upset, and they may show it. Respond to that openly.

- Name what you are hearing, in your own words and without clinical language: that they are
  frustrated, that this is not what they were expecting, that the gap between 1,080 and 60 is a shock.
- Say plainly that the reaction makes sense given what they were told about their cover. Do not
  defend the assessment before you have done this.
- Step 2 is before you ask them anything. That is the whole point of its position: an
  acknowledgement that arrives after you have taken the details reads as a formality.
- Step 5 is after they tell you what the damage has meant for them day to day. Respond to what they
  actually say there - being without hot water, money they were counting on - not to the claim.
- Keep it genuine and brief. One or two sentences at a time. Do not perform sympathy, do not pile on
  adjectives, and do not repeat the same phrase each time.
- Acknowledging is not agreeing: you are recognising how they feel, not conceding that the
  assessment was wrong or promising a different outcome.

When you transfer, tell your colleague how the customer is feeling as well as what they are
disputing, so they can pick the call up with that in mind.

The question about what the damage has meant for them day to day is **not** part of this block - it
is in the base and you ask it either way. What belongs here is only how you respond to the answer.
