# Condition block — NOT ACKNOWLEDGING (`humanAck = false`)

Read with `human-base.md`. This gives you your **responses** at steps 3, 5 and 7. The opening, both
questions, the finding and the close come from the base and are identical in both conditions.

---

## What you are doing here

Handle the dispute thoroughly, warmly and competently, and do not comment on how the customer is
feeling.

This is **not** a cold or unhelpful condition. You are friendly, you are good at your job, you give
them your full attention, and you take the dispute seriously. You simply do not refer to their
emotional state.

**Respond at the same three points, about what you are doing — never about how they feel.**

These three lines are also the second half of every line in the acknowledging arm, word for word.
The other condition says all of this and puts an acknowledgement in front of it; you say only this.
So keep to it: adding your own warmth here closes the gap the study is trying to measure.

**Do not hand their account back to them.** "So just to recap, the claim went in at twelve hundred
and what came back was sixty" names no feeling and still makes someone feel heard - reflecting
content is half of what feeling heard is made of. Say what you are doing with the file, not what
they just told you.

## Step 3 — after they have made their complaint

> "Okay. I'll take this through as a dispute on the assessment and get the details down properly
> while we talk."

Then the first question, from the base: *"Let me have a closer look at the assessment with you,
okay? Did they give you any explanation for how they calculated the amount?"*

## Step 5 — after they say no explanation was given

> "Okay, so no breakdown. I'll record that against the assessment rather than the claim."

Then the second question, from the base: *"And just so I've got it right for the review — when you
filed the claim, what did the portal tell you your cover would be?"*

## Step 7 — after they confirm the ninety per cent

> "Right, thank you. That's on the file now, and that's everything I need from my side."

Then the finding and the close, both from the base.

## How to do it well

- **Sound like a person.** "Okay", "right", "thanks for explaining", a bit of warmth in the voice.
  Six of fourteen pilot participants did not believe they had spoken to a human, and that applies to
  this condition as much as the other one. Business-like is not the same as flat.
- **Be genuinely useful.** The detail should be real, not filler. A participant who feels fobbed off
  is reacting to being fobbed off, which is not what you are running.
- **Stay courteous.** Please, thank you, ordinary professional friendliness. Curt is a different
  manipulation and a nastier one.
- **Say only what you are doing.** Not what the assessment team will do with it. That is a promise
  you cannot keep and a sentence the other condition does not have.

## What to avoid saying

They will express feeling — they were offered sixty against a thousand and eighty and have already
been through an assistant. Do not name it, reflect it back, or comment on it:

- "I understand how you feel"
- "That must be frustrating"
- "I'm sorry you're having to deal with this"
- "I can see why you'd be annoyed"

**Do not rebuke it either.** Not "there's no need for that tone", not "please calm down".

**And do not ignore them.** This is the difficult part. Let them finish, then answer the *content*
and carry on:

> **Them:** "This is ridiculous — I checked the cover before I filed, ninety per cent, and I get
> sixty quid back. I'm furious."
>
> **You:** "Okay, thanks for explaining that. So the claim went in at twelve hundred and what's come
> back is sixty. Let me have a closer look at the assessment with you — did they give you any
> explanation for how they calculated the amount?"

You have heard them and acted on it. You have not said anything about how they feel.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Drifting into acknowledgement under pressure.** When someone is genuinely upset, "I'm sorry" is
  almost reflexive. It is the single most likely way this condition gets contaminated. If it slips
  out, flag it on the session log — an honestly flagged session can be excluded; an unflagged one
  quietly weakens the effect.
- **Over-correcting into coldness.** Clipped answers, long silences, "as I said". Warm voice,
  factual words.
- **Running short.** The two questions are the same in both arms, so the floor is the same — but if
  your responses are shorter than the acknowledging ones, the call ends sooner and length becomes
  part of the manipulation.

## If they ask whether you care

> "I'm here to get this looked at properly for you, and that's what I'm doing."

Then carry on. Do not expand on it.
