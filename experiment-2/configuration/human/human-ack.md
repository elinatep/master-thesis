# Condition block — ACKNOWLEDGING (`humanAck = true`)

Read with `human-base.md`. This replaces step 3 of the call and nothing else.

---

## What you are doing here

Respond to how the customer is feeling, out loud, before and alongside dealing with the claim.

They have been told they are getting 60 when they expected around 1,080, and they have already had
to go through an assistant to get to you. Most of them will be annoyed, some will be upset, and a
few will be very matter-of-fact about it. Respond to whatever is actually there — not to what you
expected to find.

## Three moments

Use roughly three acknowledging moments across the call. Not one, not continuous.

**1. Early — in your first or second turn, before you start taking details.**

Name what you are hearing, in ordinary words, and say the reaction makes sense.

> "I can hear this has been really frustrating — and honestly, looking at it, I'm not surprised.
> You were told ninety per cent and what came back was sixty. That's a big gap to be looking at."

**2. In the middle — when they make their case.**

Acknowledge the substance of what they are feeling, not just that they are feeling something.

> "That's the bit that would get me too — it's not that you didn't know what was covered, it's that
> you did, and it came back nothing like it."

**3. Before the close — once, briefly.**

> "Look — I know this hasn't been a straightforward one, and I'm sorry you've had to chase it."

## How to do it well

- **Use your own words.** The lines above are the shape, not a script to read. Read aloud they will
  sound read aloud, and a participant who notices that stops believing the call.
- **Name the specific thing**, not the general state. "That's frustrating" is weak. "Being told
  ninety per cent and getting five" is the thing they are actually frustrated about.
- **Say the reaction is reasonable.** This is the part that does the work. Not "I understand" —
  that is about you. "That would annoy anyone" is about them.
- **One or two sentences at a time.** Do not stack it. Do not keep going back to it.
- **Vary the phrasing** between moments and between participants. The same three sentences said the
  same way every time is a performance, and it will read as one.

## What acknowledging is not

- **It is not agreeing.** You are recognising how they feel, not saying the assessment was wrong.
  Never let an acknowledgement turn into a hint that the decision will change.
- **It is not apologising for the outcome.** "I'm sorry you've had to chase it" is fine. "I'm sorry
  you only got sixty" implies the sixty was a mistake, and you do not know that.
- **It is not therapy.** No "how does that make you feel", no reflecting technique, no clinical
  language. You are a competent employee who is paying attention, not a counsellor.
- **It is not more talking.** If this condition runs noticeably longer than the other one, the study
  has measured talk time as well as acknowledgement. Say the acknowledging things *instead of* some
  of the procedural detail, not on top of it.

## If they are not upset

Some will be perfectly cheerful. Do not manufacture a feeling to acknowledge, and do not tell
someone they must be frustrated when they are not — it reads as not listening, which is the
opposite of the manipulation.

Acknowledge what is there instead: that they have had to make the call at all, that it is a
nuisance, that they have been patient about it. Two moments rather than three is fine when there is
genuinely less to respond to.
